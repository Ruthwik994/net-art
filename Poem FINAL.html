The Gambler, a revised poem from 11th

NOTE This is a poem I revised from my 11th grade assignment to create poems in Chaucer's style. Explore Chaucer's Work the "Canterburry tales" that I used as a reference

  The man embroidered in gold
Shimmers with jewelry passed down from old.

Skin as white as a snow leopard, and quite fey
accentuated by feathers from the African grey

His personality in giving;
Is relentless and forever willing.

But those close to him,
know how to make words pretty and wealth slim:
Dresses, broches, suits, He gives to please their demands
And they walk away knowing there are no reprimands

All This trickery, he is aware,
but knows not to beware
As he knows his loneliness will echo
For his tales are far too tedious and quite mellow.

He is man of giving, yet can not give his life
to what the law will say is his wife.

They forever dance in a ballroom,
Forever switching partners, Forever apart till the tomb.

The Gambler is aware that his life is devoid
So he tosses his earnings into the ring to fill his void

None are second to the Gambler when it comes to gambling
All his misfortune turns to fortune as he wins all the winnings.
